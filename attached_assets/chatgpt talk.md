I think you’ve discovered something important that wasn’t fully covered in the previous brief.
The credit system should not merely mirror FlowPay’s billing percentage. It should mirror FlowPay’s actual economic cost and revenue model depending on the transaction route.
Right now, you have two very different situations:
Scenario A: True Non-Custodial / External Gateway Split
Example:
Parent pays 100,000 FCFA.
Gateway automatically splits funds.
School receives its share.
FlowPay receives its fee.
Money never enters FlowPay’s wallet.
In this case:
FlowPay cannot reliably collect its fee through custody.
Credits make perfect sense.
Credit deduction should replace the fee collection mechanism.
This is the scenario your previous brief focused on.


⸻


Scenario B: Semi-Custodial Flow (Current Campay Model)
Example:
Parent pays 100,000 FCFA.
Money enters your Campay wallet.
Campay charges you.
FlowPay orchestrates the payout.
FlowPay disburses 100,000 FCFA to the final recipient.
Campay charges you again on disbursement.
Here:
You are paying costs twice.
Collection cost.
Disbursement cost.
This is different from true peer-to-peer.
The problem is that if you only deduct credits based on the incoming transaction fee, then FlowPay absorbs the payout cost itself.
That becomes a direct loss.


⸻


My Recommendation
Do not make credits mirror only the FlowPay fee.
Make credits mirror the FlowPay chargeable event.
There are three independent charge categories:
Category 1 — Revenue Fee
What FlowPay earns.
Example:
3% platform fee.
This is your normal billing model.


⸻


Category 2 — Collection Cost
What providers charge when collecting money.
Example:
Campay deposit fee.
Mobile money collection fee.
Card processing fee.


⸻


Category 3 — Disbursement Cost
What providers charge when sending money out.
Example:
Campay payout fee.
Bank transfer fee.
Mobile money withdrawal fee.


⸻


What Should Happen
For a true peer-to-peer gateway:
Credit Deduction
=
Platform Fee Only
Because FlowPay never pays collection or payout costs.


⸻


For Campay-style orchestration:
Credit Deduction
=
Platform Fee
+
Collection Cost
+
Disbursement Cost
Because those are real costs incurred by FlowPay.


⸻


Architecturally
I would not hardcode this into credits.
Instead introduce:
Billing Components
Platform Revenue Fee
Provider Collection Fee
Provider Payout Fee
Then the billing engine determines:
Total Chargeable Amount
Credits simply deduct that result.
This keeps credits simple.
Credits become:
Credits = Billing Result
instead of
Credits = Platform Fee Only


⸻


What I Would Tell GPT-5.5
The previous implementation directive should be refined.
The credit engine should not mirror only the platform revenue percentage.
Instead, credit consumption must be based on the final billable cost generated by the FlowPay billing engine.
Depending on the transaction route, the billing engine may include:
Platform revenue fee.
Collection/provider fee.
Payout/disbursement fee.
Other explicitly configured provider costs.
For true non-custodial transactions, only platform revenue may be charged.
For orchestrated transactions where funds pass through FlowPay-controlled wallets before disbursement, both collection and payout costs must be included.
The credit system should deduct the final billable amount rather than a fixed platform percentage so that FlowPay never absorbs provider costs and remains economically sustainable regardless of transaction route.
